# Creative work

Load for a composition, story, design, or other creative approach.

## Preserve the brief

Inspect the user's existing sketches or drafts first. Record medium, audience, intended effect, length, production constraints, and what must remain original. Do not turn the most popular reference into the specification.

## Sources

Use authored craft essays, artist interviews, process notes, primary works, exhibitions, and relevant community critiques. Name the specific structural problem: sustaining tension in one room, communicating through gesture, arranging contrast, or managing revision. A showcase demonstrates an artifact; it rarely establishes the process, budget, or audience response that produced it.

[Poe's composition essay](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/philcomp.htm) advocates designing toward an effect and ending. [George Saunders's process essay](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2017/mar/04/what-writers-really-do-when-they-write) describes discovery through revision and specificity. Their approaches can conflict; neither is an empirically established universal procedure.

## Compare and decide

Compare techniques against intent, medium, deadline, room for experimentation, and cost of reworking the artifact. Distinguish an author's report from a general rule and your proposed application from an observed result. Record rejected options and the assumptions that could change the choice.

Adopt a craft technique, extend an existing draft, compose compatible methods, or build an original treatment. The artifact may remain wholly original even when its process learns from precedent. Suggest a small trial and audience feedback; mark both pending until performed.

Learning how a reveal or revision works is distinct from copying expression, scenes, distinctive characters, assets, or another author's voice. Route copying, adaptation rights, and unclear licenses to `licensing-and-payback`.
