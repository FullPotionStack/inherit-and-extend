# Worked example: a limited statistics study path

Illustrative scenario, **not executed** with a learner and not a recorded model trial. Primary course/publisher pages and research abstracts were inspected on **2026-10-02**; the constraints, proposed plan, diagnostic questions, and decision below are author-created. There are no measured learning gains, model comparisons, or completed assessment results.

## Constraints

A hypothetical adult learner wants to interpret confidence intervals and simple hypothesis tests in reports, not qualify for a mathematical statistics course. They can rearrange algebraic equations but have not studied calculus. They have four weeks, with three 45-minute sessions each week, need free material, and want to study mainly from text rather than video. No actual diagnostic has established that their algebra is sufficient. They want a personal reading/practice plan, not a republished textbook.

Success would mean explaining uncertainty and assumptions in an unfamiliar report without relying on its headline. Completing a course or memorizing terminology is not the acceptance criterion. This limited target excludes comprehensive mastery of probability, regression, and research methods.

## Search scope

Local learner work and an existing syllabus would come first in a live task. None is supplied in this illustrative scenario; that absence is stated, not filled with invented test results. External inspection was limited to the OpenStax preface, the MIT OCW 18.05 Spring 2022 syllabus, and the two learning-research abstracts listed below. Discovery queries included "OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e preface algebra prerequisites" and a query for Dunlosky's learning-techniques review; summaries here come from the retrieved sources, not those search snippets.

Paid tutoring, other introductory curricula, learner forums, and the full research papers were not inspected. There is enough evidence to compare these options' prerequisites, but not to rank every statistics resource or certify a complete four-week sequence.

## Examined options

1. OpenStax *Introductory Statistics 2e*. The publisher says it assumes intermediate algebra, emphasizes application, and spans a one-semester introductory course. Its preface lists confidence intervals and hypothesis testing, examples and practice, and available answer resources. It warns that chapters are not independent when resequenced. This fits the stated background better than an advanced course, but the full book is too broad for this brief.
2. MIT OCW 18.05, Spring 2022. Its official syllabus lists multivariable calculus as a prerequisite and includes probability, Bayesian inference, testing, intervals, and regression. It points to interactive components on MIT's Open Learning Library. Those are useful features, but they do not remove the prerequisite mismatch. The interactive components were not tried here.
3. A custom plan of reading summaries and flashcards. This gives control over the schedule but requires designing and checking explanations and feedback from scratch. No inspected source establishes it as preferable. Flashcard recall alone would not demonstrate interpretation of a report.
4. Selected OpenStax material plus independently written retrieval prompts and later review. This composes existing explanations with a narrow practice schedule rather than writing a new textbook. Its sequence still needs a prerequisite check and adjustment based on actual attempts.

## Lessons and failure modes

The primary Roediger and Karpicke study abstract describes prose-learning experiments: repeated study did better on the immediate test, while prior retrieval testing did better on delayed tests, despite increased confidence after rereading. The lesson for this proposal is to avoid mistaking immediate familiarity for later independent performance. It is not proof that a particular statistics exercise schedule works.

Dunlosky and colleagues' review rates practice testing and distributed practice as high-utility across examined conditions, and describes weaker or less general evidence for several other techniques. This is a research synthesis, not a primary experiment or validation of the suite. The abstract is the evidence inspected here; exact schedules, feedback protocols, and statistical transfer effects were not checked in the full paper.

A possible failure is skipping prerequisite probability concepts to reach the desired chapters quickly. OpenStax itself cautions against treating chapters as independent. Another is choosing MIT for its reputation and discovering that calculus demands consume the available time. These are constraint-based risks, not observed learner failures. A plan based only on highlights and completed pages would also miss the stated interpretation goal.

## Bounded decision

Compose a personal plan around selected OpenStax readings, contingent on an initial algebra/probability check. Use the first week for sampling and descriptive uncertainty, the second for prerequisite probability and distributions, the third for confidence intervals, and the fourth for simple tests and integrated interpretation. This is a proposed ordering, not a verified curriculum; use prerequisite sections even when that means dropping a later topic.

In each session, attempt an original question before opening notes, consult a linked explanation, then explain or solve a related unfamiliar case. Revisit earlier material in a later session. Example original prompts: "What missing sampling information would stop you trusting this interval?" and "Why can two studies reporting the same sample mean justify different confidence in a conclusion?" They are not copied textbook exercises.

After the first week, review actual attempts and time spent. If algebra or probability blocks explanations, extend prerequisite study and narrow the final goal; do not preserve the deadline by pretending readiness. At the end, use an unfamiliar report and explain assumptions and uncertainty after a delay. Administration, scoring, and feedback remain pending. If the original user request were to create the plan, hand this decision back and finish that authorized artifact; do not stop at a shopping list of courses.

## Learning versus copying

This example learns from published prerequisites, sequencing cautions, and research findings. It links to material instead of reproducing its teaching text, figures, or answer keys. OpenStax's inspected page states CC BY-NC-SA terms and also posts permission notices for AI ingestion and commercial printing. Do not infer that free access or an open license permits every use in an agent, adaptation, or redistribution. Verify the exact work's terms and notices with `licensing-and-payback` before such use; this example does not assess those notices' legal enforceability. These summaries do not relicense the sources.
